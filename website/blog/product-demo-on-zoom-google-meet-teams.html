How to give a product demo on Zoom, Google Meet or Teams

November 16, 2026 · David

In a room, a demo has your full attention and theirs. On a video call, you're one tab among twenty on someone's screen, and the moment they lose the thread, they're reading email.

Whether you're a sales engineer, a founder pitching a customer or a product manager showing a new feature, most demos now happen over Zoom, Google Meet or Microsoft Teams. The software is the same as in person. What changes is how easily people get lost.

This guide covers how to set up, run and follow up on a product demo over video, with the settings that matter in each app.

Why remote demos lose people

  • The screen is too busy. A real product has menus, sidebars, tables and badges. Shared at full size, every viewer looks somewhere different.
  • Nobody can see what you're pointing at. Your cursor is small, and depending on what you share, it may not be captured at all.
  • Text is too small. You're on a large monitor. Your prospect is on a laptop with your demo shrunk into half the window next to the video tiles.
  • Things go wrong live. A stray click opens the wrong page, a notification slides in, or customer data you didn't mean to show is on the screen.

Set up your demo before the call

  1. Use a demo account with realistic sample data. Real-looking data makes the product feel real. Empty states and “test test 123” don't.
  2. Plan two or three workflows, not a feature tour. Pick the jobs this audience cares about and show those end to end.
  3. Open every page in its own tab, in order. Switching tabs is faster and safer than navigating live.
  4. Use a clean browser window or profile. No bookmarks bar full of personal links, no unrelated tabs, no extensions popping up banners.
  5. Turn on Do Not Disturb and quit chat apps you don't need for the call.
  6. Do a dry run with a colleague on the same meeting app, and ask them to watch on a laptop, not a big monitor.

Settings for Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams

The rule is the same everywhere: share one window or one tab, never the whole screen. The details differ a little.

Zoom

Click Share Screen and pick your browser window rather than the whole screen. If your demo plays video or sound, tick Share sound before you start. Ask attendees to use side-by-side view or to maximise the shared screen if text looks small.

Google Meet

If you run the demo in Chrome, choose to share a Chrome tab rather than a window or your entire screen. Tab sharing gives the smoothest video and can include the tab's audio. You can switch to another tab with the share-this-tab option instead of stopping and starting again.

Microsoft Teams

Click Share and choose a window instead of the screen. Turn on Include sound if you need audio. If you present often, a dedicated browser window for the demo makes it easy to find in the list.

During the demo: show one thing at a time

The best remote demos feel like a guided tour, not a screen dump. That comes down to controlling where people look.

Blur everything except what you're talking about

With Revlur, you drag a box around the part of the product you want to show and the rest of the page is blurred. The audience still sees the layout, so they understand where the feature lives, but there's nothing else to read. When you move on, press Re-select and drag around the next part. Because the blur is on the page itself, it shows up the same in Zoom, Meet, Teams or anything else.

Make the important part big

Press Zoom in Revlur's toolbar and the selected area is enlarged over the blurred page, so a small chart or form is readable on a laptop. Since it's a snapshot rather than browser zoom, the page layout doesn't reflow underneath you.

Point so people can see it

In Zoom view, a red pointer dot follows your mouse. It's drawn on the page, so it appears in the screen share even when the meeting app doesn't capture your cursor. “This number here” finally means something.

Protect the demo from mistakes

  • No accidental clicks. The blurred area ignores clicks, so you can't open a record or a menu outside the part you're showing.
  • No jumping page. While Revlur is on, the page holds still. Press Scroll when you want to move down.
  • No surprise data. If you have to demo in a real account, blurring the rest of the page keeps other customers' names and numbers unreadable.

Talk less, pause more

After each workflow, stop and ask a question: “Is this how your team does it today?” Remote audiences rarely interrupt, so you have to make room for them.

After the demo

Follow up the same day with a short recap and a few screenshots of the moments that landed. In Revlur's Zoom view you can copy or save the enlarged area as an image, so you can grab exactly the chart or screen that got a reaction and paste it straight into your email.

Questions

Should I share my whole screen or one window for a demo?

One window or one browser tab. It keeps notifications and other apps off the call. In Google Meet, sharing a Chrome tab also gives the smoothest video and lets you share the tab's audio.

Why can't my audience see my cursor during a demo?

Depending on the app and what you share, the cursor isn't always captured, and when it is, it's small. A pointer drawn on the page itself, like Revlur's red dot in Zoom view, shows up in every meeting app.

How do I hide customer data during a live demo?

Use a demo account with sample data where you can. When you have to show a real account, blur everything except the area you're presenting.

Does Revlur work with Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams?

Yes. Revlur changes the web page, not the meeting app, so the blur and pointer show up in any screen share: Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Webex or Slack huddles. It works on web apps in Chrome, not on desktop apps.

How long should a product demo be?

Aim for 15 to 20 minutes of actual demo, built around the two or three workflows this audience cares about, and leave the rest of the meeting for questions.

The short version
