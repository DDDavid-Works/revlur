How to present a dashboard without losing your audience

November 8, 2026 · David

A dashboard is designed for one person scanning many numbers. A presentation is many people looking at one number. That mismatch is why dashboard reviews so often drift.

The problem

Put a full dashboard on a shared screen and every person in the room reads something different. One is on revenue, one has spotted a red number in the corner, one is trying to read a table that's too small on their laptop. By the time you've made your point, the questions are about three other charts.

Before the meeting

  • Decide on three things. Pick the few numbers or charts that carry your message. Everything else is supporting detail you show only if asked.
  • Set the filters first. Choose the date range, segment and comparison before the call, so you aren't clicking through menus while everyone waits.
  • Close everything else. Share only the browser tab with the dashboard, and turn off notifications.

During the meeting

Show one thing at a time. This is the most important habit. Start with the headline number, explain it, then move to the chart that explains why. The simplest way to do this on a live dashboard is to blur everything except the part you're talking about.

With Revlur you drag a box around the chart, and the rest of the dashboard is blurred. People still see the layout, so they know where they are, but there's nothing else to read. When you're ready for the next point, press Re-select and drag around the next chart.

Make small numbers readable. Dashboards are dense, and on a shared screen the labels are often unreadable. Revlur's Zoom enlarges the selected area over the blurred page, and a red pointer dot follows your mouse so you can point at a specific bar or row. The dot is drawn on the page, so it shows up even when the meeting app doesn't capture your cursor.

Don't let the page move. While Revlur is active the page is held still, so a nudge of the trackpad won't scroll your chart away. Clicks on the blurred area do nothing, so you can't accidentally open a drill-down halfway through a sentence.

After the meeting

If someone asks for “that chart”, open Zoom on it and use the copy or save button to grab it as an image, ready to paste into a chat or follow-up email.

The short version

Prepare three points, share one tab, and show one thing at a time. For more ways to direct attention on a call, see how to highlight one part of your screen while screen sharing.
